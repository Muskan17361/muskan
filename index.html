Popular destinations

Hand-picked places across Japan with what to see and when to go.

Tokyo

Metro hub

Neon nightlife, shrines, shopping in Shibuya and Harajuku, Michelin ramen, and contemporary culture.

Kyoto

Timeless temples, tea houses, geisha districts and seasonal cherry blossoms.

Osaka

Food capital — takoyaki, okonomiyaki, lively Dotonbori and friendly locals.

Hokkaido

Wider skies, lavender fields, winter skiing and fresh seafood.

Sample 5-day itinerary

Day 1 — Tokyo: Arrival & Shinjuku

Arrive in Narita/Haneda, get a Suica card, explore Shinjuku Gyoen and dinner in Omoide Yokocho.

Day 2 — Tokyo: Culture & Modernity

Meiji Shrine, Harajuku crepes, teamLab Borderless (or Planets), then Shibuya Crossing at dusk.

Day 3 — Hakone: Onsen & Views

Ride the Romancecar or Shinkansen + local transport, take a lake cruise and relax in an onsen (public onsen etiquette applies).

Day 4 — Kyoto: Temples & Gion

Fushimi Inari, Kiyomizu-dera, stroll through Gion and look for traditional tea houses.

Day 5 — Osaka: Food & Departure

Morning in Dotonbori, lunch of takoyaki, head to Kansai/Itami airport for departure or extend to Hiroshima/Nara.

Travel essentials
  • JR Pass: only worth it if you take multiple long-distance trains (reserve before arrival).
  • Cash: many places accept cards but smaller shops and shrines prefer cash.
  • Etiquette: quiet voice on trains, remove shoes when requested, queued boarding.

Travel tips & seasonal highlights

Best time to visit

Spring (late Mar–Apr) for cherry blossoms, autumn (Oct–Nov) for foliage. Summer festivals and winter ski season in Hokkaido.

Food to try

Sushi, ramen, udon, kaiseki, okonomiyaki, wagashi (traditional sweets) and regional specialties.

Connectivity

Rent a pocket Wi‑Fi or buy a local SIM. Many stations have luggage forwarding services (takkyubin).
